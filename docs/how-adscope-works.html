AdScope tells you which websites your advertising should appear on — by predicting the questions your target audience asks AI assistants, and then discovering which websites those assistants rely on to answer.

The shift

Why this exists

For years, deciding where to advertise meant relying on a planner's experience, traffic reports, and audience panels. That still matters — but how people discover products has changed.

Increasingly, people don't start with a search engine or a magazine. They ask an AI assistant — ChatGPT, Gemini, and others — questions like "What's a good organic sunscreen for daily use in India?" The assistant answers by drawing on a set of trusted websites, and often names them.

That creates a new, high-value question for advertisers: when my audience asks an AI assistant about my product category, which websites does that assistant surface? Those sites shape the customer's decision at the moment of intent — and AdScope finds them systematically instead of guessing.

The idea

A simple thought experiment, run at scale

AdScope does three things, in order:

Imagine
Picture your customer talking to an AI assistant. What would they actually type?
Ask
Put those same questions to AI assistants and note which websites they'd consult to answer.
Rank
Collect every website and rank them. The ones that keep appearing are where your brand most wants to be seen.

That's the whole concept. Everything else is about doing this reliably, transparently, and with a human in control.

01

You describe the campaign

Fill in a short form: client, campaign, target country, objective, an optional budget, and a free-text briefing describing the product, audience, and positioning.

Example brief A premium organic skincare brand targeting women aged 25–40 in major Indian cities. Objective: brand awareness.
02

AI drafts the questions your audience would ask

AdScope sends the brief to several AI models at once and asks each: "What would a real person in this audience type into their own AI assistant?" Each returns a set of realistic questions — about 30 in total across three models, covering research, comparison, buying, how-to, and local angles.

Example questions "What are the best premium organic skincare brands in India right now?" · "Which face serums work well for humid monsoon weather?" · "Where do beauty editors recommend shopping for skincare online?"
03

You review and refine the questions Human step

AdScope pauses and shows you every drafted question in a clean editor. You can edit the wording, remove ones that don't fit, deselect some to keep on record, or add your own. Nothing proceeds until you submit — the AI proposes, you decide.

04

AI finds the websites for each question

For every question you approved, AdScope asks each AI model: "To answer this, which websites would you consult?" Each returns a scored list. Ten approved questions across three models means thirty separate website lists — gathered automatically in the background while a progress screen keeps you posted.

05

AdScope ranks everything into one list

All those lists are merged into a single ranked list of publishers — what you present and act on. How the ranking works is next.

Reliability

Why several AI models, not one

Any single AI model can be biased or incomplete. AdScope deliberately asks multiple independent models and looks for agreement — the same principle as consulting several experts and trusting what they concur on.

If three models all name a website, that's a strong signal. If only one does, it's weaker — worth noting, not over-weighting. AdScope is also extensible: it isn't locked to a fixed set of models. New AI providers can be added as they emerge, without redesigning the system.

The ranking

Two simple ideas decide the order

No maths required to explain either one.

Idea 1 — Agreement

For a single question, if several models independently name the same website, they agree. More agreement means more confidence the site is genuinely relevant to that question.

Idea 2 — Breadth

A site that appears for many of your audience's questions is broadly important — it sits at the centre of the conversation, not just one corner of it.

AdScope prioritises breadth first, then agreement, then relevance score. In plain terms: a website that keeps appearing across many customer questions, that several AI models agree on, ranks at the top. Every website carries both signals so you can see exactly why it ranked where it did:

Queries
How many of your questions surfaced this site (breadth).
Models
The most AI models that agreed on it within a single question (agreement).
Breadth
A High / Medium / Low badge — a quick read on how widely it appeared.
Score
An overall relevance score from 0 to 100.

Worked example

The skincare campaign, all the way through

  1. Brief. Premium organic skincare, women 25–40, Indian metros, awareness.
  2. Drafted questions. ~30 across three models — from "Best organic skincare for women in their 30s in India?" to "Which websites review clean beauty brands?"
  3. Your review. You keep the 12 sharpest, tweak two, and add one of your own: "Which sites review vegan skincare?"
  4. Website discovery. For each of those 13 questions, three models each list the websites they'd consult.
  5. Final ranking. Vogue India and Nykaa appear for almost every question and are named by all three models — so they top the list. Niche sites that appeared once or twice rank lower. You export and present.

The output is defensible: for any website, you can show the exact questions and models behind it.

Flexibility

Tuned to the situation, no rebuild needed

Three dials adjust how much ground AdScope covers:

Questions
How many questions each model drafts (default 10).
Websites
How many sites each model returns per question (default 10).
List size
How long the final list can be (default 50).

These can be set per campaign on the form, or globally in configuration. Bigger numbers mean broader coverage but longer runs — a simple speed-versus-thoroughness dial. A built-in Demo Mode runs the entire flow with realistic sample data and no live AI calls — ideal for demonstrations and training without incurring cost.

Trust

Advisory and auditable, by design

Human-in-the-loop. The system never runs end to end without your review of the questions. You steer what it investigates.

Full traceability. Every recommendation traces back to the specific questions and models that produced it.

Resilient. If one model fails or is slow, the others still produce results; a run only fails if everything fails, and partial gaps are flagged as "completed with warnings."

Honest about its nature. Recommendations are AI-generated and advisory. Suitability, audience data, pricing, availability, and brand safety must be verified by a qualified professional before any spend. AdScope does not invent traffic numbers or pricing.

Think of AdScope as a very well-read research assistant that rapidly surfaces where the conversation is happening — with a human expert always making the final call.
